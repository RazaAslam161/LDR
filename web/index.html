For two

Built for exactly two

No feed. No followers. Just the two of you.

Pairing happens by invitation between two people. There are no profiles to browse, no discovery, and no public surface at all — nothing either of you shares is visible to anyone else. And there are no ads and no sale of your data: private connection tools for couples, that's the whole business.

Security

Private by design

End-to-end encryption, where it applies
Memory Threads, Wish Jar entries, message reactions and the messages written during a separation are sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 using keys held on your two phones — the server stores ciphertext it cannot open. Chat and the Personal Vault are not among them: they are encrypted in transit and at rest and reachable by nobody else, but the operator of the database could read them. Section 2 of the privacy policy lists both sides in full.
App lock & covers
Biometric lock, plus optional covers — alternate launcher identities, named honestly in settings — for phones that get handed around.
Invite-only, always
Pairing happens by invitation between two people. No profiles to browse, no discovery, no public surface at all.
Your life is not the product
We never sell your data, and nothing you write, send or store is used to target advertising. Where the app's ability to act is limited, our documents say so — they are written to be accurate rather than reassuring.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

Can anyone find me on Miles?

No. There is no directory, no search, and no public profile. The only way two people connect is a private invitation code shared between them.

Can Miles read our messages?

Your Memory Threads, Wish Jar entries, message reactions and the messages written during a separation are end-to-end encrypted — those we cannot open. Chat and the Personal Vault are not: they are encrypted in transit and at rest and reachable by nobody else, but the operator of the database could read them. The privacy policy lists exactly what falls on each side.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Your account recovers by email, and Miles keeps a sealed copy of your encryption key so that reinstalling does not destroy your history. That seal is derived from your account password — the same password you sign in with — so anyone who has that password could open it. Section 3 of the privacy policy sets out the trade.
