# Addiction slate — 2026-08-15

Generated by a 9-agent tournament: six mechanic lenses (loss-aversion, reciprocity,
variable-ratio, synchrony/scarcity, investment/economy, caretaking/collection), three
judges scoring compulsion / N=2-safety / feasibility independently. Extends — never
duplicates — the 2026-08-15 product brief (Rerun, Handoff, Sealed, Box, Overlap,
Tucked, Ember, Drift). Scores: C = raw compulsion, N = N=2 viability & coercion
safety, F = feasibility on this stack. All three 1-10.

The organizing insight, from the brief and confirmed by every judge: at N=2 the
slot machine is the PARTNER — their timing, mood, attention. These mechanics
amplify that variance and route it through the archive, while keeping absence
illegible so no mechanic can be quoted in a fight (a fight uninstalls two users:
coercion IS the churn mechanism here).

## The Pull  —  C8 N9 F7 (Σ24)

*Rerun's daily card grows a handle: one private pull per person per day on a rarity ladder over the couple's own archive — commons, deep cuts, paired sets, and a pity-timed Lost Chapter jackpot, with drop rates tuned server-side after ship.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: at local midnight the Home card re-cocks to its unpulled state (appointment scarcity; no push, no badge). ACTION: a pull-down gesture with a ~1.5s reveal whose shimmer color telegraphs tier mid-animation — a telegraph that resolves to a common IS the near-miss, tuned to ~30% density. VARIABLE REWARD: deterministic draw hash(coupleId, dayNumber, tableVersion) against an app_config drop table — COMMON ~70%: one recent artifact (today's Rerun); DEEP CUT ~20%: an item 1+ year old never resurfaced; PAIRED SET ~8%: two auto-matched artifacts (same date across years, a prompt answered twice, a photo plus the message sent about it); LOST CHAPTER ~2% with hard pity at day 45: a full scrolling replay of one high-density day from their past — messages in order, photos, the 2h14m call row. Both partners who pull the same day draw the SAME card. INVESTMENT: Rerun's Keep/Again verbs pin marks onto the drawn artifact so its next draw is richer; every artifact the couple makes anywhere loads the drum.

**Psychology.** Variable-ratio reinforcement proper — the daily open is a lever pull with unknowable payout tier; near-miss engineering via the reveal telegraph (Clark et al. 2009: near-misses recruit striatal reward circuitry; Kassinove & Schare 2001: ~30% near-miss density maximizes play persistence); pity-ceiling anticipation (a jackpot that MUST arrive by day 45 makes droughts escalate instead of extinguish); endowment effect — every payout is their own memory, so the reward cannot ring hollow.

**Stolen from.** Genshin Impact's pity system — hard pity at 90 pulls, soft pity from ~74; community wish-log datasets (millions of logged pulls) show the effective 5-star rate leaping from ~0.6% to 20%+ inside the soft-pity band, i.e. the ceiling concentrates play, on a model that grossed $4B+ on mobile. Plus Facebook Memories — 90M+ daily users of resurfaced own-content (Facebook, 2018) — proof the archive is jackpot-grade material.

**N=2 adaptation.** No crowd loot pool needed: rarity is manufactured from archive DEPTH (age, never-resurfaced, cross-year pairing), so payouts are the couple and cannot be faked. The shared deterministic seed makes it a two-player slot with one outcome — 'did you pull today?' becomes a conversation the app never has to prompt. Thin-archive guard: below an app_config row-count threshold the ladder compresses and seeds from chat photos, so month-one couples still hit deep cuts. Pity advances per calendar day, not per pull, so skipped days cost nothing.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** The streak-shaped danger: 'you didn't pull today' as a nightly audit. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: pull state is absolutely private — no partner-visible indicator, no shared pity meter, no makeup pulls, and pity ticks on calendar days rather than pulls, so absence leaves zero residue and zero lost progress while the daily draw stays unknowable; all the variance survives untouched.

**Build cost.** Small-medium; the reveal animation is the bulk. One app_config row (JSON drop table + version) read at startup; one resurfacings table (couple_id, day, artifact_kind, artifact_id, tier — Rerun needs it anyway for no-repeat); Lost Chapter is a client-side day-bucket query over messages/gallery; one gesture + animation widget on the Home card. No push, no edge function, no native code; copies the promptForDay deterministic idiom (daily_prompt_repository.dart:33).

**Composes with.** Extends Rerun — its selection engine and Keep/Again verbs ARE the common tier. Draws from gallery, chat, daily_prompt responses, reasons, capsule, memory_threads, visits, and the call/watch session rows the brief's Hours-emission half endorses; supplies Handoff's never-empty morning fallback; Crossings and Almanac cards enter the drum as rare candidates.

> **Compulsion judge (8/10):** The most complete pure schedule in the set: daily midnight re-cock (appointment), tiered variable-ratio draw, near-miss telegraph at the empirically persistence-maximizing ~30% density, hard pity making droughts escalate rather than extinguish, and endowed payouts (your own memories) that cannot ring hollow. The craving moment is exact — first phone check of the day, 'is today a deep cut?' — and the shared seed makes the draw conversational. Deducting for zero loss (skipped days deliberately cost nothing) and common-tier staleness risk; otherwise this is Wordle-grade architecture with casino-grade internals.

> **N=2 judge (9/10):** Fully solo and fully private: no partner-visible pull state, no shared pity meter, pity ticks on calendar days so skipped days cost literally nothing — absence leaves zero residue and zero lost progress, which is the exact shape this dimension rewards. Local-midnight re-cock is timezone-trivial. The shared deterministic card is a conversation gift, not evidence. Residual is content-level, not mechanic-level: a Lost Chapter can replay a high-density day that was a bad day, and the design names no retirement valve for it here. One point off for that.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** Deterministic draw, app_config drop table, plaintext day-bucket queries over messages/gallery — all cheap and update-channel-safe. But it 'extends Rerun' and Rerun does not exist: the selection engine, resurfacings table, and Keep/Again verbs must be built first, plus the reveal animation is admitted as the bulk. Closer to 1.5-2 weeks than days.

## The Vow (streak wager with an escrowed stake)  —  C6 N9 F8 (Σ23)

*You seal a gift you have already made into escrow and bet it against your own next 7 days: only consecutive days of showing up for your partner release it — and failure is invisible to everyone but you.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: private flame chip on Home showing YOUR open vow and days remaining (partner's client never renders it), plus an evening local notification (generic 'News · 1 update' text, disguise-safe, scheduled on-device). ACTION: any qualifying act toward the partner that day — chat send, prompt answer, reason added, handoff seal, tuck — auto-detected from writes the repositories already make, so the vow adds zero new chores, it charges existing ones. VARIABLE REWARD: day-completion is fixed, but the terminal reward is the brief's own scarce resource — the partner's unscripted reaction to a windfall they cannot explain; and for the whole vow window every reply from them is charged with private stakes they don't know about. INVESTMENT: the stake is sunk creative work (a recorded voice letter, a photo set) locked before day 1; each completed day raises the cost of abandoning; on failure the stake is NOT destroyed but cold-stored for 14 days, after which re-staking it is the re-entry hook — Forest's dead tree appears only in your private ledger, never theirs. One silent grace day per vow is built in (freeze-as-near-miss). Max one live vow per person.

**Psychology.** Loss aversion on sunk work + Ulysses-contract pre-commitment + Zeigarnik open loop (an escrowed thing of yours is waiting to be released). The wager is strictly against your future self, which is the only opponent Rule Zero permits.

**Stolen from.** Duolingo's Streak Wager: offering it produced statistically significant D1/D7/D14 retention gains, D7 the largest at +14%, with wagered users returning more often and completing more lessons (Duolingo blog). Forest's dead-tree mechanic proves stake-you-created loss aversion interrupts automatic behavior — with the documented caveat that its novelty fades in 4-6 weeks, which is why the stake rotates (each vow requires making a NEW artifact, so the wager loop also feeds the archive).

**N=2 adaptation.** At N=2 the classic wager's public accountability is the coercion vector, so it is amputated: vows, failures, the chip, and the cold-storage ledger are owner-RLS rows the partner's client cannot query. Delivery arrives through the ordinary chat/Sealed path with NO mechanism label — completed-vow gifts are indistinguishable from spontaneous ones, so the receiver just experiences unpredictable windfalls (their own variable-reward loop, debt-free per the Drift principle: nothing was asked of them).

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** A partner who learns the feature exists can demand 'make vows for me' or interrogate 'did you fail one?' — and self-punishing users can guilt-spiral. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: no verification handle exists — deliveries are unlabeled and failure renders as literally nothing, so the demand cannot be enforced or audited; meanwhile the compulsion (sunk days + your own locked artifact) is entirely sender-side and survives untouched. Cap one active vow, silent grace day, stake never destroyed.

**Build cost.** One table `vows` (id, user_id, couple_id, stake_kind, stake_ref, ciphertext for the note, target_days, days_done, last_tend_local_day, grace_used, state) with owner-only RLS (negative test: partner account selects zero rows). Stake sealing reuses crypto_core/key_escrow + existing vault storage; tend-detection is one hook in the supabase_repository write path with the qualifying-act list read from app_config (server-tunable, no client update needed); flutter_local_notifications ^22.0.1 is already in pubspec.yaml. No edge function, no cron. Screens: create-vow sheet, Home chip, completion moment. ~3-5 days.

**Composes with.** chat + Sealed send paths (delivery), vault/closer_crypto (escrow), daily_prompt/reasons/handoff/tucks (as qualifying tends), Home. The tend-detector is shared infrastructure with The Garden below — build once, consume twice.

> **Compulsion judge (6/10):** Real loss teeth: sunk creative work in escrow plus accumulating days replicates Duolingo's wager (+14% D7 is real evidence), and the craving moment is nameable — 10pm, flame chip lit, no qualifying act done yet. Two structural leaks cap it: qualifying acts are things active users do anyway, so the loop only bites on marginal days; and re-entry requires authoring a NEW artifact per 7-day vow, a chore that will decay exactly like Forest's 4-6 week novelty fade. Strong bursts, questionable months-long repetition.

> **N=2 judge (9/10):** Fully solo: the wager is against the self, owner-RLS private, and failure renders as literally nothing on either phone — no counter, no receipt, no quotable fault. Deliveries are unlabeled, so even a partner who knows the feature exists cannot audit or enforce a demand. Timezone-trivial (local days). The only residual is self-directed guilt on a failed vow during the vower's own busy week — a user-experience cost, not a relationship weapon. Grace day + non-destroyed stake soften it. Docked one point because the interrogation vector ('did you fail one?') exists once the feature is discovered, even if unenforceable.

> **Feasibility judge (8/10):** One owner-RLS table, escrow via existing crypto_core/key_escrow + vault storage, flutter_local_notifications already in pubspec, no cron/edge fn. Ding: tend-detection hook must touch many repository write paths (chat, prompt, reasons exist; 'handoff seal' and 'tuck' do not), and app_config must be created first (trivial additive migration on the app_release pattern). Realistic 4-6 days.

## Flare  —  C6 N8 F9 (Σ23)

*One luminous send, granted on unpredictable days, expiring unused at local midnight — Wordle's daily ration applied to expression, with the server's randomness doubling as the alibi that keeps Rule Zero intact.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: the daily check — did the app grant me a Flare today? A nightly cron grants each USER (independently, privately, p≈0.6, tunable via app_config) one Flare slot; grants are visible only to the holder. ACTION: spend it — any one message, photo, or voice note sent as a Flare renders luminous on both phones for 24 hours: distinct bubble, warm light, the one thing in the stream that provably cost the day's only shot. Unspent slots expire at local midnight, silently, unbanked. VARIABLE REWARD: three-sided — the holder's grant is intermittent (checking whether you HAVE one is a variable-ratio pull before any send happens); the receiver's day may or may not contain one (a Flare arriving mid-Tuesday is a jackpot precisely because most days don't); and what the partner chose to spend theirs on — of everything they could have said — is the reward with teeth: scarcity is what converts a message into a judgment of worth. INVESTMENT: at 24h the glow fades and the Flare becomes an ordinary archived message — but it stays flagged in metadata, making Flares first-class candidates for Rerun and guaranteed front-page items in The Sunday Paper: the day's one shot compounds into the record of what mattered.

**Psychology.** Commodity theory — a rationed signal carries value an unlimited one cannot, which is why 100 free texts feel like less than one Flare — layered on a variable-ratio grant schedule (the check-for-availability is itself the reinforced behavior) and loss aversion on the expiring unused slot (11pm with an unspent Flare is a designed itch).

**Stolen from.** Tinder's Super Like — the category proof that rationing one send-signal per day inflates its value: Tinder's official launch data claimed a Super Like makes a match 3x more likely and the conversations it starts last 70% longer; the free ration of one per day is the entire mechanism. Wordle proves the expiry half of the compulsion: one per day, gone at midnight, built a 3M-DAU habit in ten weeks.

**N=2 adaptation.** Tinder's ration allocates scarce attention across a crowd; at N=2 there is exactly one possible recipient, so the scarcity stops being allocation and becomes pure devotion-signaling — the question 'who gets it' is answered, leaving only 'what is worth my one shot today', which is a better question for a couple than any streak. And the intermittent grant does double duty unique to N=2: because availability is private, per-user, and random, no partner can ever prove the other HAD a Flare and withheld it — the server's dice are every quiet day's alibi. Both phones on both nightstands means the midnight expiry gives the day a soft closing bell.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest risk: this is tribute-shaped — 'where's my Flare' as a daily tax is the streak pathology wearing jewelry, and a controlling partner could demand the day's shot be spent on command. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: unverifiable intermittent supply plus zero ledger — grants are RLS-visible only to the holder, the app never confirms to anyone what slots the partner had ('I didn't get one today' cannot be checked, by construction), there is no Flare history, count, or calendar anywhere, and after 24h a Flare is just a message — so the tribute demand has no scoreboard to enforce itself against and every non-send is deniable weather. The scarcity-value survives untouched because it lives in the grant schedule, not in any visible record.

**Build cost.** The cheapest here. Server: flare_slots(user_id, local_day, spent_at) with holder-only RLS, granted by a nightly pg_cron insert (shares Aurora's cron infrastructure if both ship); one small RPC to atomically consume a slot on send. Client: one composer affordance + one luminous bubble treatment in chat_screen.dart, a kind:'flare' metadata flag on the existing messages write path (additive, parses as ordinary message on any older client — sideload-safe by construction), midnight expiry is pure client logic off ServerClock. Tunables (grant probability, glow duration) in app_config. Rollback: stop the cron, feature renders as ordinary messages.

**Composes with.** chat (lives in the existing stream, not an eleventh silo), Rerun (flagged Flares are premium pool candidates), The Sunday Paper (the week's Flares are automatic front-page items), Handoff (a Flare sealed into the night is the obvious crossover), disguise (a generic 'News' notification spoils nothing), and app_config (the grant dice are server-tunable forever without touching the fleet).

> **Compulsion judge (6/10):** Two nameable daily moments — the morning 'did I get one?' grant-check (variable-ratio on availability itself, a genuinely novel placement) and the 11pm unspent-slot itch (real expiry loss). Tinder's Super Like ration is fair evidence that scarcity inflates signal value. What holds it below Wordle: the reward for the holder is permission to compose, which on empty days converts to mild obligation ('don't waste it'), and the receiver's jackpot arrives passively in a chat they were opening anyway. Solid teeth, modest jaw.

> **N=2 judge (8/10):** Tribute-shaped by the idea's own admission, but the defusal is structural: grants are private, random, and unverifiable, so 'I didn't get one today' can never be checked and every non-send is deniable weather — the demand has no scoreboard. Solo mechanic, local-midnight expiry, no AND-gate, no ledger, no history surface. Residual: received Flares live in chat scrollback and human memory, so a long drought is statistically legible ('you basically never Flare me') even though each day is individually deniable; the alibi thins over months. Well short of a receipt, hence 8.

> **Feasibility judge (9/10):** Cheapest of the set and it checks out: messages.kind column exists so the additive 'flare' kind parses as an ordinary message on old clients (sideload-safe), holder-only RLS, nightly pg_cron insert on existing cron infra, one consume RPC, one bubble treatment in chat_screen.dart, ServerClock midnight logic. 2-3 days, rollback is 'stop the cron'.

## Fluency (the archive as a decaying skill)  —  C4 N9 F9 (Σ22)

*A strictly private spaced-repetition deck auto-minted from the couple's own archive — her prompt answers, the reasons, the dates — where your fluency in your partner visibly frosts over when unpracticed and the due pile is a debt only you can see.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: a private due chip ('4 due') on Home/games visible only to its owner; lapsed cards frost over visually — decay rendered as ice on your own memory, never as a score. ACTION: a 2-minute review — attempt recall ('what did she answer to this, March 2025?'), reveal the real row, self-grade honest/hazy/gone; SM-2-lite reschedules. VARIABLE REWARD: the reveal IS the reward — a random resurfaced fragment of the couple's history, usually forgotten, drawn from tables nothing currently reads back; occasionally a card resolves to 'ask them tonight' — a live retrieval where the partner unknowingly becomes the answer key, converting a private rep into a real conversation. INVESTMENT: every review extends intervals (deck health is accrued work); lapsing compounds the pile (Anki's inbox-zero debt, self-inflicted and self-visible only); and every new prompt answer, reason, and visit auto-mints future cards — living in the app funds the deck, so the deck makes every OTHER surface's writes worth more.

**Psychology.** Skill-decay framing (Duolingo's own model: skip days and the ability itself erodes) + due-pile completion compulsion + the testing effect — retrieval practice physically strengthens the memory it touches, so the compulsion loop is also genuinely deepening the user's knowledge of their partner, which pays off outside the app where the app can't and shouldn't measure it.

**Stolen from.** Anki's due-pile compulsion and Duolingo's decay framing, grounded in Roediger & Karpicke 2006: one week out, retrieval practice retained 61% of material vs 40% for re-reading — recall beats resurfacing by half again. That is the measured argument that Fluency is not redundant with Rerun: Rerun is passive re-exposure, Fluency is active retrieval over the same archive, and retrieval is what makes it stick.

**N=2 adaptation.** Duolingo's corpus is a language; here the corpus IS the partner, and the two users sleep next to each other's phones — so the entire surface is owner-private: no score exists anywhere (frost is per-card and unquantified), reviews leave no trace the partner's client can render, and the payoff is designed to surface VERBALLY in real life ('you remembered') rather than in-app, which is the one reward channel Rule Zero can never contaminate. Cards are app-generated only from structured rows; closer/cycle data is excluded by default.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Two vectors: 'show me your deck' (a demandable screen), and the sharper one — partner-AUTHORED cards ('things you should remember about me') would be a standing demand surface with a compliance test attached. Mitigation that keeps the compulsion: authored cards are excluded by design v1, and no aggregate number exists to demand or compare — there is nothing to show but frost — while the due-pile debt loop, which is entirely self-visible, keeps full force. Ship authored cards never, or only behind mutual opt-in with no review-visibility either way.

**Build cost.** One table `card_states` (user_id, couple_id, card_key, ease, interval_days, due_local_day) with owner-only RLS; cards themselves are deterministic client-side projections of existing rows (prompt_responses, love_reasons, visits, memory_threads metadata — the exact tables the brief says are written and never read), so no content duplication and E2EE surfaces stay untouched. One review screen + SM-2-lite (~100 lines) + minting rules in app_config (server-tunable qualifying sources). No server logic, no push. ~3-4 days.

**Composes with.** daily_prompt/reasons/timeline/memory_threads (finally a second read path over them, complementing Rerun), Rerun itself (a long-lapsed card can BE the day's Rerun card — one surface, two retrieval modes), games tab (natural home), app_config.

> **Compulsion judge (4/10):** The due-pile is homework wearing a heart: Anki's compulsion is real only for the already-disciplined minority, and its unforced churn is notoriously brutal — Duolingo retains via streak and gamification, not via SRS itself. The reveal-a-forgotten-fragment reward is genuinely nice but arrives on a fixed schedule with no loss (frost is cosmetic, the pile just grows, which reads as debt-avoidance, i.e., a reason NOT to open). Hard to name a craving moment that isn't 'I should do my reviews' — obligation, not itch.

> **N=2 judge (9/10):** Entirely owner-private and solo-driven: no shared state, no score, no aggregate, nothing the partner's client can render, so 'show me your deck' finds only unquantified frost. Partner-authored cards — the real demand surface — are excluded by design. Dies gracefully in the owner's own busy week (a due pile, private). Timezone-irrelevant. The payoff surfaces verbally in real life, which is unweaponizable. Docked one point only because reviewing a partner's memories and grading them 'gone' is emotionally loaded material sitting one lock-screen glance away on a shared nightstand — a discovery risk, not a mechanic flaw.

> **Feasibility judge (9/10):** One owner-RLS card_states table; cards are pure client-side projections of tables that exist today (prompt_responses, love_reasons, visits, memory_threads metadata); SM-2-lite is ~100 lines; no server logic, no push, no painter. Reads E2EE content only after local decrypt, so E2EE holds. The 3-4 day estimate is credible on this stack.

## The Cellar  —  C6 N8 F8 (Σ22)

*A dead-drop shelf where their deposits ripen at server-tuned random moments; the cellar door on Home is a lever you pull — and the payout schedule lives in app_config, tunable in production despite the update-channel-less sideload fleet.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: the cellar door on Home, rendered IDENTICALLY at all times — one jar silhouette whether 0 or 10 deposits wait (the silhouette is decoration, not data), so any moment could be a ripening and no moment proves emptiness. ACTION: open the door — a deliberate check, the lever pull. VARIABLE REWARD: either a ripened deposit — their voice, a photo, a line, made days or weeks ago, aged into a small time capsule — or the same warm nothing, which never renders empty: a Rerun card appears instead (the Handoff trick). INVESTMENT: finds prompt restocking the partner's cellar by mood rather than debt (no reply affordance on a jar — Drift's rule); found jars take Rerun's Keep mark; every deposit thickens the archive. Each deposit gets an opaque server-side ripe_after drawn from a hazard distribution whose parameters (mean interval, daily find cap, circadian jitter) are rows in app_config — the ONE genuinely tunable variable-ratio schedule this fleet can have: if checking runs too lean or too rich, retune the payout server-side, no APK.

**Psychology.** Variable-ratio reinforcement on the CHECK itself — the Skinner-box lever, the schedule slot machines and pull-to-refresh run on — plus a standing curiosity gap: you know deposits CAN exist and can never know the count. Pull, not push: the compulsion is 'something might have ripened', never 'I owe an acknowledgment'.

**Stolen from.** Snapchat's unopened-snap glyph — the most compulsive icon in mobile, the check-in loop Snap's open-frequency numbers were built on — crossed with pull-to-refresh, which its own designer (Loren Brichter) and the Hooked literature describe explicitly as a slot-machine schedule. The correction Miles makes: Snapchat's glyph LEAKS state (no-glyph is legible absence, exactly what Rule Zero forbids); the Cellar's constant silhouette keeps Snapchat's schedule while destroying its legibility.

**N=2 adaptation.** At crowd scale a mystery inbox is spam; at N=2 every jar is from the one person whose voice you want, so even a lean schedule pays out emotionally. Timezones make the schedule BETTER, not worse: they stock during your night, so your morning check after their evening is the richest pull — the reward schedule acquires a natural circadian texture nobody had to engineer, and the wake-up check becomes the ritual. Receiver silently caps finds per day, including zero (Drift's rule), and the sender is never told a cap exists.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest: the residual channel is out-of-band interrogation — 'did you get the jar about X, why didn't you say anything' — and a checking habit pointed at a partner's deposits can turn a lean week into felt evidence of not being loved. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: total sender blackout PLUS silent expiry — unfound deposits evaporate after N days with no trace on either side, so the app will never confirm a specific jar was served, and 'did you get it' has no answerable referent; the sender cannot build the audit trail the interrogation needs. The check loop — the entire compulsion — is untouched; only the evidence is destroyed. Empty-shelf legibility is already dead by construction (constant silhouette, Rerun fallback).

**Build cost.** One table cellar_items(id, couple_id, from_user, ciphertext, kind, created_at, ripe_after, found_at, expires_at). RLS: receiver SELECTs only rows where ripe_after < now() — the server clock arbitrates, same refusal pattern as capsule_items_select_unlocked, so a patched client cannot peek; sender can never SELECT found_at (column excluded from their policy). Two or three rows in app_config for schedule parameters, read at startup exactly as release_gate.dart already reads app_release. One Home door widget, one reveal screen, composer reuse. Roughly 3-4 days.

**Composes with.** Drift (this IS Drift's stockpile with the delivery loop inverted from push to pull — the fix for Drift's stated 'refill has no reward' bet, because finds visibly justify restocking), Rerun (never-empty fallback + Keep verb), Handoff (ripenings during their night queue into The Night), ServerClock, and the disguise — a 'News' app the owner compulsively checks is the perfect cover story.

> **Compulsion judge (6/10):** Receiver-side this is a textbook variable-interval lever — constant silhouette, unknowable count, never-empty fallback — the pull-to-refresh schedule aimed at the one voice that matters; the morning check after their evening is a nameable daily craving. But the supply side is starved by the design's own safety rails: total sender blackout plus silent expiry means restocking receives zero reinforcement ever, so the shelf runs dry on faith and the check loop extinguishes against Rerun consolation prizes. A slot machine whose coin hopper nobody is paid to refill.

> **N=2 judge (8/10):** Both halves are solo: sender stocks on mood, receiver pulls on curiosity; neither requires the other's same-week attention. The constant silhouette kills empty-state legibility, sender blackout plus silent expiry destroys the audit trail ('did you get it' has no answerable referent), and timezones improve the schedule. Residual: over weeks, a receiver who never finds jars will infer a lean sender — the cap/expiry ambiguity blunts but cannot fully erase the statistical read, and 'a lean week as felt evidence of not being loved' is the idea's own admission. Illegible enough to stay at 8.

> **Feasibility judge (8/10):** One table, ripe_after < now() RLS is exactly the shipped capsule unlock pattern, app_config schedule params read at boot like app_release, composer reuse, no cron. Two wrinkles: hiding found_at from the sender needs column privileges or a view (RLS is row-level only), and the never-empty Rerun fallback references an unbuilt surface — droppable for v1. 3-5 days.

## The Mint  —  C8 N8 F6 (Σ22)

*Gacha packs whose loot table is the couple's own archive: acts of affection earn pack tokens, pulls mint their real moments into rarity-tiered cards, and everything files into ONE shared binder with silhouetted set slots that want completing.*

**Loop.** Trigger: a banked private token + an idle moment, or an unopened gifted pack landing on your phone. Action: the pull ritual — hold-to-tear reveal animation, three cards minted on-device from the archive via hash(couple_id + pack_id) so the draw is deterministic but unpredictable to the human. Variable reward: WHICH moments surface (a random Tuesday message rendered as a common is still YOUR memory — gacha with no trash tier, which fixes the resentment problem every real gacha has) and what rarity lands: rarity computed from true properties — first-ever photo = legendary, 3am messages = night foil, both-answered prompts = holo pair, the 1000th message = milestone, artifacts marked Keep in Rerun = auto-holo. Investment: the shared binder fills sets ('Firsts', 'Nights', 'The Long Distance', month-long seasonal banners defined in app_config — new sets forever without an APK; unfinished seasonal sets persist as silhouettes and REOPEN next year, Panini-style), duplicates auto-upgrade finish, and earning more tokens requires generative acts toward the partner — the grind loop's fuel is literally tending the relationship. The affection move: instead of opening a pack, seal it and send it — your partner gets the reveal dopamine, sender-blind, no receipt, no reply affordance.

**Psychology.** Variable-ratio reinforcement (pack contents) + completion compulsion with engineered near-miss (8-of-9 set slots is the single most documented compulsion state in collection design) + endowment squared (the loot pool is their own history, so possession value is pre-installed) + sunk cost (the binder as accumulating switching cost) + reciprocity WITHOUT debt via the gifted reveal — the gift is a transferred reward, not an obligation.

DECAY: living layer wilts on a 5-7 day half-life, drawn from dew reserve FIRST; permanent structure is never touched; floor state is asleep-not-dead; one act fully relights.

Every property Ember correctly removed is re-added here in an illegibility-engineered form rather than raw.

**Stolen from.** Duolingo: streak drives ~2x daily retention (Deconstructor of Fun teardown), 7-day-streak users 3.6x likelier to stay long-term, and — the load-bearing one — Streak Freeze reduced churn 21% among at-risk users: forgiveness buffers INCREASE retention. Animal Crossing's weeds prove players return to tend a place they built. Snapstreak is the dyadic proof of compulsion — and its counter is precisely the organ this design amputates.

**N=2 adaptation.** The streak's three quotable organs are removed and each replaced: the NUMBER becomes a place (no figure to quote — Hours-refusal compliant); ATTRIBUTION is impossible at the schema level (no user_id, the Ember trick); the CLIFF becomes cosmetic wilt (nothing permanent is ever losable, so nothing can be 'broken' and no one can be blamed for breaking it). The freeze bank is the genuinely new move: at N=2 its job is not monetization but NOISE — because dew absorbs quiet days invisibly, the garden's state never maps 1:1 to any specific day's behavior, so wilt is not evidence about any particular absence.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** 'The garden is wilting and that's you' — subtraction attribution, the residual the brief admits for Ember and Overlap. The mitigation that keeps the compulsion: the dew reserve makes that claim unprovable in-app (display is decoupled from behavior by an invisible buffer of unknowable size), while the grind that FILLS the reserve adds a loop instead of removing one; and since decay never touches permanent growth, there is no hostage a partner can hold. Rare-variant draws stay unattributed — the garden never says whose act grew the golden fern.

**Build cost.** One table `garden_days` (couple_id, local_day, acts smallint, PK couple+day) + one upsert RPC; local_day computed device-side per the RoutineRepository.today() pattern. All state derives deterministically client-side from the row set, so both phones render identically with no server logic. Half-life, dew rates, and rare-variant odds read from app_config (server-tunable post-ship). Monthly pressing = render CustomPainter to PNG → existing gallery_items insert. The real cost is the procedural garden renderer (CustomPainter + species generator) — the largest UI lift of these four ideas: ~1-2 weeks, of which ~2 days is everything except the painter.

**Composes with.** Occupies and upgrades Ember's Home slot (the brief's designated streak-replacement slot); shares the tend-detector with The Vow; deposits pressings into gallery → Rerun; app_config for tuning; the renderer is reused wholesale by Bloom Days.

> **Compulsion judge (6/10):** Daily appointment with a variable species draw ('what sprouts today') plus permanence endowment is a genuine loop, and the Home-backdrop trigger fires on every open. But the anti-coercion engineering guts the very organs that make Duolingo a 9: no number, no cliff, and the invisible dew reserve means the user can't feel how close to wilt they are — the 11:50pm streak panic that powers Duolingo cannot form when your own jeopardy is illegible. Cosmetic-only loss that one act fully reverses is loss aversion at homeopathic dosage.

> **N=2 judge (8/10):** OR-gated and schema-level unattributed (no user_id): either partner alone keeps it green, so visible wilt is joint fault by construction — the accuser could always have prevented it themselves, which structurally defuses 'that's you.' Dew decouples display from any specific day. Nothing permanent is losable, so no hostage exists. Residual: it is still a shared decaying surface, and a fight doesn't need proof — visible wilt starts the conversation even if the app makes the attribution unprovable. Cosmetic-only decay and joint-fault-by-construction keep it at 8, not lower.

> **Feasibility judge (6/10):** Schema side is near-perfect (one couple+day table, deterministic client render, app_config tuning, RoutineRepository.today() pattern exists) — but the procedural CustomPainter garden renderer is 1-2 weeks by the idea's own admission, the largest UI lift in the set. Everything except the painter is ~2 days; the painter alone pushes it out of 'days'.

## Bloom Days (perishable appointment scarcity, no crowd required)  —  C5 N8 F7 (Σ20)

*On one or two unpredictable days a week — a schedule both phones compute from a hash, no server involved — a flower opens in the garden for 24 hours; either partner presses it into the permanent collection, or it vanishes without record and never existed.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: on a bloom day the garden glows on app open, plus one day-of local notification (generic disguise text; the schedule is hash(couple_id, weekIndex)-deterministic so the client knows it offline — zero server, zero push infrastructure, and per the brief's Overlap correction, nothing presence-derived that could act as an awake-detector). The 24h UTC-anchored window covers both partners' waking hours across timezones. A countdown shows only while the bloom is live, framed as the flower's own lifespan — a deadline that accuses nobody. ACTION: either partner alone taps to press it; optional 10-second voice or one-line mark attaches. VARIABLE REWARD: species and rarity are revealed only on pressing — procedurally unbounded space, rare golden variants — a variable-ratio draw gated behind a scarce appointment. INVESTMENT: pressed blooms accumulate in a permanent collection with no slots, no totals, no dates — only what exists is shown, so the shelf never displays a gap; each pressing carries its mark into the Rerun candidate pool, compounding the archive. LOSS: an unpressed bloom vanishes at window close with NO record — you never learn what you missed.

**Psychology.** Appointment scarcity + anticipated regret (loss looms BEFORE the event, is invisible AFTER — the asymmetry that keeps loss aversion but starves the post-hoc accusation) + variable-ratio rarity + open-ended collection compulsion with the completion-meter deliberately removed.

**Stolen from.** Pokémon GO Community Day — the monthly limited-hours event with exclusive moves and ~1/25 shiny odds is the canonical miss-it-and-it's-gone appointment (structure documented on Bulbapedia; the DAU spike is widely reported but I found no first-party number, flagging as unquantified). Duolingo's monthly badges run the same forever-gone scarcity. The N=2 insight: these events work per-player, not per-crowd — the crowd was never the mechanism, the deadline was, and a deadline ports to two people.

**N=2 adaptation.** Crowd events need servers and crowds; here the event is a pure function both phones agree on offline — the same determinism trick promptForDay and the Overlap bag already use. Either-alone-suffices kills the reciprocal-obligation shape: one keen partner can keep the collection whole while the other free-rides, and the keen one is exactly the retention target. Pressing is unattributed (the row stores no user_id) — the keepsake belongs to the couple, so 'who pressed it' is not a fact the app possesses. Misses are unrecorded, so the couple cannot be put on trial for them, jointly or severally.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** 'Did you press today's bloom?' as a recurring demand, or guilt over jointly missed windows. The mitigation that keeps the compulsion: either-alone + no attribution means the nag has no target and no audit — the anxious partner can always just do it themselves — while the perishability (the compulsive organ) is untouched. Schedule is only discoverable day-of, so it cannot be turned into a posted duty roster. Residual stated honestly: 'we missed one' is inferable by whoever watched the countdown expire; what the design guarantees is that the app never confirms it, never counts it, and never shows the hole.

**Build cost.** Near-zero server: one table `bloom_presses` (couple_id, bloom_id, mark ciphertext, created_at — no user_id) with couple-RLS. Client: the schedule function (~30 lines, promptForDay idiom), day-of scheduling via flutter_local_notifications (already a dependency), collection screen, and species rendering that reuses The Garden's painter — ~3-5 days if the Garden ships first, ~2x standalone.

**Composes with.** The Garden (blooms live and glow in it; shared renderer), Rerun (every keepsake + mark is a candidate artifact), gallery/capsule (keepsake storage), the promptForDay/NoRepeatBag determinism idiom, flutter_local_notifications.

> **Compulsion judge (5/10):** The perish-forever-with-no-record loss is elegant and the rarity draw on pressing is a real variable reward, but the cadence self-limits: 1-2 events per week with a day-of notification produces roughly two prompted opens weekly, not a daily itch — the notification does the work checking would otherwise do. Community Day is the right analogy and it drives spikes, not dailies. 'You never learn what you missed' protects Rule Zero but also amputates post-miss regret, half of the loss engine.

> **N=2 judge (8/10):** Either-alone-suffices is exactly right at N=2: the anxious partner can always press it themselves, so the nag has no target. Misses are unrecorded, the collection shows no gaps, presses are unattributed, and the 24h UTC window handles timezones. Survives one partner's busy week entirely on the keen partner. Residual, honestly stated in the idea: whoever watched a live countdown expire carries the memory 'we missed one' — the app never confirms it, but the live countdown is a visible deadline that can generate day-of pressure pings. Minor and self-resolvable, hence 8.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** Near-zero server (one no-user_id table, couple RLS), deterministic hash schedule is the proven promptForDay idiom, local notifications already a dependency. But its 3-5 day estimate assumes The Garden's painter ships first; standalone it doubles because the species renderer must be built. Feasible either way, days-to-a-week-plus.

## Crossings  —  C5 N7 F8 (Σ20)

*When both partners do the same kind of thing at nearly the same moment without planning it — messages crossing in flight, reaches colliding, the same word typed twice — the server mints a rare fused artifact; every act of affection is a lottery ticket against the partner's genuinely unpredictable timing.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: invisible — the machine rides acts the couple already makes. ACTION: sending anything (message, photo, voice note, reach, prompt answer, reason) is buying a ticket; zero new UI on the send path. VARIABLE REWARD: a pg trigger detects matching-class acts from both partners inside a per-class window (app_config: ~3 min for crossed messages, ~60 min for photos, same-evening for prompt answers) that are not replies to each other; hits mint a Crossing — both artifacts fused on one gilded card stamped only 'within moments of each other'. Tiers priced by natural frequency: message crossings common, photo crossings rare, reach-for-each-other collisions jackpot with a once-only fused animation, same-word echoes (server-readable chat) ultra-rare. The mint is revealed on both phones at the NEXT DAY boundary, never live. INVESTMENT: Crossings are permanent archive objects that feed The Pull's rare tier; a private shelf shows silhouettes of crossing TYPES not yet earned — a completion set with no numbers on it.

**Psychology.** Variable-ratio where the RNG is the partner themselves — the one genuinely unpredictable reward source at N=2; each affectionate act is a wager with a small real chance of a jackpot that cannot form without the partner's uncoordinated echo, so more affection = more tickets with no ask ever transmitted. Layered with set-completion compulsion (the silhouette shelf) and the jinx effect: evidence of simultaneous thought is among the highest-valence signals in a relationship, and this is the first messenger ever to notice it.

**Stolen from.** BeReal — one unpredictable synchronized moment per day drove ~20M daily actives at its 2022 peak (app-intelligence estimates): manufactured simultaneity compels ritual engagement. Snapchat Friend Emojis/Charms — badges minted automatically from real dyadic interaction patterns, the scaffolding Snap layered around streaks precisely because behavior-minted tokens between two specific people carry weight no generic reward does.

**N=2 adaptation.** At crowd scale coincidence is constant and worthless; at N=2 a genuine cross-in-flight is rare and MEANS something — the emotional payload is real and the app only notices it (WhatsApp holds this exact metadata and will never spend it). Per-class window widths are the RTP dial in app_config: a couple whose timezone offset starves them of crossings gets windows silently widened server-side, post-ship, no client update — exactly what a sideload fleet needs. A couple that colludes ('both send at 9:00') is co-playing their own slot: harmless, arguably the point.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Two honest vectors. (1) A minted Crossing proves the partner was active at some moment — mitigated by minting ONLY from acts already mutually visible (never presence, never app-opens), blurring stamps to 'moments apart' with no clock time, and delaying the reveal to the day boundary so exact timing cannot be reconstructed. (2) 'We never get Crossings because you never text first' — mitigated by the shelf showing only types earned, never counts, rates, or dates-since; Crossings cannot be lost, so there is no decay to blame anyone for. The compulsion — every send is a ticket — is untouched by either mitigation.

**Build cost.** Small-medium. One crossings table (couple_id, kind, artifact_a, artifact_b, tier, minted_for_day); one shared SQL detector function + AFTER INSERT triggers on messages/reach_events/gallery_items/prompt_responses (timestamps and kind are plaintext even where payloads are E2EE; the word-echo class is restricted to server-readable chat; closer modules excluded entirely); app_config window rows; one card widget + one shelf screen. No push needed.

**Composes with.** chat, reach, photo, daily_prompt, reasons as ticket sources; Sealed gets one safe crossing class ('you both sealed within the hour' — seals are already visible as weights); mints feed The Pull's rare tier, Rerun, and Almanac's compound-anomaly class.

> **Compulsion judge (5/10):** Making the partner the RNG is the smartest single trick in the batch, and every-send-is-a-ticket charges existing behavior at zero cost. But judged as an open-driver it is thin: the trigger is invisible, hits are minted at day-boundary only, jackpots are rare by construction, and there is no loss and no appointment — it amplifies sending and adds one mild morning check ('did we cross?'). A brilliant overlay for other loops, a weak standalone lever.

> **N=2 judge (7/10):** Rides acts the couple already makes, never renders misses, cannot decay, and mints only positive joint evidence — the failure mode is silence, not accusation. Survives a busy week with zero residue (nothing shows, nothing is lost). But the reward itself is AND-gated on uncoordinated simultaneity, so a mismatched-rhythm couple gets structural droughts, and the silhouette shelf of unearned types is a standing visible gap that at N=2 resolves by subtraction ('we never get the reach crossing because you never reach'). The jackpot classes require the partner's behavior specifically, which makes the want demand-shaped even though the surface never testifies. Positive-only and unlosable keeps it at 7.

> **Feasibility judge (8/10):** Timestamps and kind are plaintext on every source table and messages.body is confirmed plaintext, so the word-echo class works; AFTER INSERT triggers + one table + app_config windows is all standard Postgres. Moderate SQL complexity (per-class windows, not-a-reply logic, day-boundary reveal via fires_on RLS) but zero client-side heavy lift. 4-6 days.

## Seasons  —  C4 N8 F8 (Σ20)

*Six-week server-defined seasons that change what the app can make — modes and prompt packs that exist only for those weeks, artifacts stamped with the season forever, each year binding into a volume. Live-service novelty with no update channel and nobody to blame for the deadline.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: season transitions (a new season visibly re-skins Home and unlocks a season-specific way of making things — e.g. Ember Season: voice-only replies after dark; Monsoon Season: photos auto-pair into diptychs; plus a season prompt pack replacing the 20-string promptPool defect server-side) and, separately, the final-week deadline gradient ('Ember ends Sunday'). ACTION: make things in the season's limited mode — ordinary sends, prompts, photos, all carrying the season's mark. VARIABLE REWARD: what the next season IS stays unknown until it arrives (server-authored novelty the sideload fleet cannot otherwise get); what the partner makes in-mode is unpredictable; and at year's end the volume compiles — a bound chapter-book of the year the couple didn't know they were writing. INVESTMENT: season-stamped artifacts are sunk cost at calendar scale — a season you sat out is a texture permanently absent from your year's volume (anticipated regret), and every completed volume raises the switching cost of the archive by exactly one irreplaceable year.

**Psychology.** Limited-time availability / commodity theory (the mode's value comes from its expiry date) + anticipated regret at the season boundary + endowment on the accumulating volumes. Critically, the deadline gradient — the most coercive force in streak design — is pointed at the CALENDAR, which ends blamelessly for both partners at once, never at the partner.

**Stolen from.** Animal Crossing: New Horizons — the definitive crowd-free proof that real-clock seasonal scarcity alone drives return visits: fish, bugs, recipes and events exist only in certain real months, and the CHI PLAY 2021 retention study found time-gated content and collection, not social features, were the significant retention factors. Fortnite's season model is the industry-scale confirmation that periodized content with hard end-dates is the strongest known re-engagement cadence in live service.

**N=2 adaptation.** Battle passes need tiers, grinding, and a crowd to compare against — all deleted. What ports to N=2 is the pure time-gate: 'this way of making things for each other exists only these six weeks.' Seasons also solve Miles's unique structural problem: a sideloaded fleet with no update channel normally means the app is frozen at install — but seasons are rows in a server table read at startup, so the app visibly CHANGES every six weeks without shipping a byte, which is the single strongest anti-staleness lever available on this stack. The volume is couple-private: at N=2 the 'collection' is the relationship's year, which both partners co-own by construction.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest risk: the final week creates pressure a partner can point ('season ends Sunday and you've put nothing in our volume') — deadline guilt by proxy. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: the volume has no per-person attribution (Ember's no-user_id trick applied to pages), no progress meter, no slot grid, and no empty-page placeholders — a thin season binds into a thin but complete-looking chapter, so there is no visible hole to litigate and no number to quote; deadline copy names only the season, never the couple's output ('Ember ends Sunday', never 'your volume needs more'). The deadline pull survives intact because it lives in the calendar, not in a meter.

**Build cost.** Cheap-medium, and the cleverest part costs nothing: a season is a pure function of created_at — no stamp column needed on any artifact table, just seasons(id, starts_on, ends_on, theme jsonb, modes jsonb, prompt_pack jsonb) read at startup beside app_release (release_gate.dart pattern already fetches at boot). Client: season theming hooks, 1-2 mode variants per season in existing composers, one volume-compiler screen (deterministic, client-side, both phones render the same book). Zero cron, zero edge functions, zero new RLS complexity (seasons are world-readable). Also quietly fixes the brief's flagged defect: the 20-string promptPool cycling every 20 days is replaced by server-delivered seasonal packs.

**Composes with.** Every write surface (chat, gallery, daily_prompt, reasons, capsule — anything with created_at is retroactively seasonal), Rerun ('this season last year' becomes a selection dimension), timeline, and Aurora/The Sunday Paper (seasons re-skin the sky-event kinds and the paper's masthead, so all three appointment mechanics share one novelty pipeline).

> **Compulsion judge (4/10):** Trigger cadence is every six weeks plus a final-week deadline — that is a re-engagement spike engine, not a daily itch, and between boundaries the season mode is just the app wearing a hat. Animal Crossing's time-gating worked atop dense DAILY loops this idea doesn't supply; anticipated regret over a thin volume is deliberately (and correctly, for safety) defanged into 'thin but complete-looking,' which removes the loss. Superb anti-staleness infrastructure for the sideload fleet; weak as a compulsion loop in its own right.

> **N=2 judge (8/10):** The deadline points at the calendar, which ends blamelessly for both at once — the safest possible target for urgency. Solo-viable: either partner's makes fill the volume, pages are unattributed, no meter or empty-page placeholder exists, and a thin season binds complete-looking, so a busy six weeks produces no artifact of absence. Timezone-trivial. Residual: 'season ends Sunday and you've put nothing in' is still speakable — the volume can't prove it, but a partner's own memory of their contributions enables subtraction, and the final-week gradient hands that grievance a date. Structurally defused, socially available: 8.

> **Feasibility judge (8/10):** World-readable seasons table read at boot beside app_release, zero cron, zero RLS complexity, and season-as-pure-function-of-created_at avoids touching artifact tables — genuinely the best exploit of server-tunable config on a fleet with no update channel. Caveat: prompt packs and palettes are tunable forever, but every future 'mode' must already exist in the shipped APK; the volume-compiler screen is the main UI cost. ~1 week.

## Understory  —  C7 N8 F5 (Σ20)

*A jointly owned living terrarium under the app — flora grows from either partner's ordinary use, and collectible creatures visit on an unpredictable schedule, catchable for BOTH by whichever partner happens to look.*

**Loop.** Trigger: the standing possibility that something is in the garden right now (visitors are live for 2-8h windows on a deterministic pseudo-random schedule both phones compute identically) — a pure variable-interval check-in cue, plus care that accrues as a side effect of acts you were doing anyway (sending a message, ticking a routine, answering the prompt all mark the couple's day as tended). Action: open the world, look, tend. Variable reward: WHO is visiting (species drawn from server-tunable spawn tables in app_config — new species shippable to the sideload fleet without an APK), what keepsake it leaves (rare visitors carry a fragment minted from the couple's own archive — a line from an old message tied in a ribbon), and whether it's a new species for the bestiary. Investment: the bestiary fills against silhouetted unmet species ('not yet' slots), flora accumulates permanently and never regresses, the keepsake shelf grows — a living object that exists nowhere else and cannot be exported. Rare species are attracted by discovered-not-instructed behaviors (a moth in the hours after a voice note; a heron in a week with a call), and winter-only species spawn in QUIET fortnights — so a slow stretch produces exclusive collectibles instead of a dying pet.

**Psychology.** Variable-interval reinforcement (the Neko Atsume check-in engine — the strongest schedule for sustained checking because reward timing is uncertain but never punished), collection/completion compulsion via silhouetted bestiary slots, endowment over a jointly-owned living thing, and caretaking motivation with the guilt half surgically removed: no hunger meter, no death, no sickness — the failure state is replaced by a seasonal palette that has its own exclusive rewards.

**Stolen from.** Neko Atsume (reported ~10M downloads in its first year on nothing but 'cats visit while you're away, check who came' — proof that check-in caretaking retains WITHOUT any death/guilt mechanic, and that unmet visitors need no fail state), Tamagotchi (~80M units on caretaking responsibility — the compulsion we keep, minus the visible-death half we amputate), Animal Crossing NH (40M+ units; real-clock visitor and season appointments drive daily opens for years).

**N=2 adaptation.** Care is OR-gated with no per-person record: world_days(couple_id, local_day) insert-if-absent, NO user_id — Ember's exact anti-scorekeeping pattern — so either partner's ordinary use tends the world and 'I do all the work' has no data to appeal to. A visitor met by ONE partner is captured for BOTH — my 2am insomnia catches the moonmoth for us, so checking becomes a gift the other wakes up to (phones on the nightstand make the night/morning check the natural rhythm, same minute Handoff owns). Unmet visitors leave ZERO trace — no visit log, no footprints, no 'you missed X' — so checking frequency is never auditable; the FOMO lives entirely in the silhouettes, which read as 'not yet', never as fault. Quiet weeks spawning winter exclusives inverts the Tamagotchi failure: absence manufactures rarity instead of damage.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest residuals: bloom intensity is still an activity meter by subtraction ('the garden's grey and it's because of you'), and 'go check the garden' can become a chore demand; a controlling partner could also monitor bestiary growth to infer when the other was in the app. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: flora is cumulative-only and never regresses (quiet weeks shift palette to winter — which has exclusive species — rather than decaying anything), and no surface anywhere renders tended/untended days, per-person acts, who met a visitor, or when — the only number in the feature is joint bestiary completion, which only rises. The check-in pull survives intact because it comes from spawn uncertainty, not from threatened loss.

**Build cost.** Largest of the four. New features/understory: world screen (layered sprites/CustomPaint, sprite sheets in-APK, no new dependency), bestiary screen, Home thumbnail card. Tables: world_days(couple_id, local_day) insert-if-absent; sightings(couple_id, species_id, met_on, keepsake_ref) with the standard couple-scoped RLS. Spawn schedule computed on-device from hash(couple_id + epoch_window) + ServerClock — no cron, no edge function; spawn tables/rarity/season rules as JSON in app_config (server-tunable despite the frozen fleet; ship N dormant species in the APK, unlock via config). One Broadcast channel understory:<coupleId> so a live 'met' celebrates on both phones simultaneously (capsule_proximity idiom).

**Composes with.** Care hooks piggyback on existing write paths (chat send, routines tick, daily_prompt answer, reasons add); ServerClock + profiles.timezone for seasons and night spawns; keepsake fragments are read off the same plaintext metadata columns Rerun samples; realtime Broadcast idiom already proven in capsule proximity; it occupies and absorbs Ember's Home slot — the world thumbnail IS the ambient warm object, with an actual reason to tap it.

> **Compulsion judge (7/10):** Neko Atsume's engine ported honestly: 2-8 hour visitor windows are generous enough that speculative checks actually pay (unlike Aurora's minutes), the 'something might be there right now' craving is nameable several times a day, silhouetted bestiary slots supply standing want, and catch-for-both makes a 2am check a gift the partner wakes to. Winter-exclusive species in quiet weeks is a genuinely clever anti-extinction move. Held from 8 by Neko Atsume's own trajectory — check-in caretaking fades as the bestiary fills — though server-extensible spawn tables partially answer that.

> **N=2 judge (8/10):** OR-gated care with no per-person record, no death, no regression, unmet visitors leaving zero trace, and the elegant inversion that quiet fortnights mint exclusives — absence manufactures rarity instead of damage, which is the single best answer in the set to the busy-week problem. One partner's 2am catch is a gift both wake to. Residuals, honestly listed by the idea: winter-palette shift is still a joint activity meter by subtraction, and new bestiary entries imply someone was in-app during a window — positive-presence inference that can start a fight of its own ('you were in the app at 2am but didn't text me'), only coarsely blunted by not rendering who or when. 8.

> **Feasibility judge (5/10):** Mechanics are all proven idioms (no-user_id world_days, deterministic on-device spawns, app_config species tables, existing broadcast channel pattern) — but it is the largest lift by its own admission: a layered sprite world screen, bestiary, and above all sprite-sheet art production, which this one-dev pipeline has no cheap source for. Multi-week; punished on build size, not on stack fit.

## The Atlas  —  C5 N8 F7 (Σ20)

*Rerun extended into fog-of-war over the couple's own history: every week they've existed is a dark tile on a growing map, a scarce weekly budget of 'expeditions' digs artifacts out of chosen weeks, and Keep lights the tile permanently — completion compulsion pointed at the archive itself.*

**Loop.** Trigger: the weekly joint expedition budget refreshing (~3/week, app_config-tunable, use-or-lose within the week — a real appointment) plus the standing sight of the dark frontier ('we've relit 34%', a number that only rises). Action: pick any dark tile — a week of their shared past — and dig; the engine surfaces one artifact from that week (Rerun's deterministic hash pick, scoped to the week), with one redraw. Variable reward: what the week actually contains — and here the genius of the mechanic at N=2: human memory is lossy, so YOUR OWN FORGOTTEN PAST IS THE LOOT TABLE — a photo neither remembers taking, the first use of a pet name, a fight that's now funny. Keep lights the tile forever with a glyph of what lit it; lit tiles open into that week's kept artifacts. Investment: contiguous lit eras auto-name into chapters ('the first winter'), completing a region mints a region card into The Mint; the map grows a new tile at the frontier every week forever, so the object is never finished but regions within it are — permanent, compounding, unexportable progress.

**Psychology.** Map-completion compulsion (fog-of-war clearing is one of gaming's most reliable self-sustaining drives — players clear map fog with no extrinsic reward attached), the reminiscence bump + rosy retrospection (old artifacts appreciate, so the loot table improves with age), appointment scarcity via the expiring weekly budget, and the Zeigarnik pull of visible dark regions in an otherwise lit era.

**Stolen from.** Zelda BOTW/TOTK and the open-world fog-of-war pattern (map completion consistently self-reported as a top driver of 100+ hour play — the compulsion needs no prize beyond the lit map), Timehop (reported ~6M daily users at peak on pure archive resurfacing) and Facebook On This Day (memories among Facebook's highest-engagement re-engagement surfaces — the brief already banks on this via Rerun; the Atlas adds the completion frame On This Day never had, which is why On This Day is a card you glance at and the Atlas is a territory you work).

**N=2 adaptation.** The territory is literally the dyad's shared past — a map only these two possess and the switching-cost argument made visible in one screen. Expeditions work as a two-player ritual (call, pick a tile together) but are deliberately solo-safe: either partner digs alone and the tile lights for BOTH, so the other opens the app to find new territory lit while they slept — asynchronous care as discovery, the same OR-gate-as-gift shape as Understory's captures. The budget is JOINT with no per-person attribution, so 'who spent our digs' has no data. Tiles are weeks, not days — coarse enough that no tile maps to a single evening someone can be interrogated about.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest: the expiring joint budget invites 'we wasted our digs again' blame (at N=2 a joint shortfall is attributable by subtraction), and expedition choice can be weaponized — deliberately digging up bad weeks to relitigate them. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: budget state renders ONLY for the current week and no history of unspent weeks exists anywhere in schema or UI (nothing to quote), and Rerun's silent retirement extends to the Atlas — either partner can invisibly retire any artifact or an entire week from the pool; retired weeks simply stay dark and expeditions never land on them, indistinguishable from 'not yet', so suppression is unreadable. The appointment pull survives because the refresh cadence, not the waste record, is what drives the ritual.

**Build cost.** Smallest of the four — it is mostly Rerun's engine re-aimed. One map screen (CustomPaint spiral/coastline of week-tiles growing from the pairing date), expedition flow reusing Rerun's selection function and Keep/marks table verbatim, one table expeditions(couple_id, week_key, artifact_ref, kept_at) plus writes into memory_revisits — the table that exists in the schema with zero code references finally gets its purpose. Weekly budget = a count query, no new table. Region names and budget size in app_config. No edge functions, no push, no native code.

**Composes with.** Built directly on Rerun (same selection engine, same Keep verb, same silent-retirement pool); feeds The Mint (region-completion cards); anchors on memory_threads.happened_on, visits, and every timestamped archive table; Handoff's morning screen can show 'a tile lit while you slept'; activates memory_revisits.

> **Compulsion judge (5/10):** Fog-of-war completion is a real self-sustaining drive and your-own-forgotten-past is a superb loot table, but the loop meters itself to weekly rhythm: 3 use-or-lose expeditions per week yields a few opens plus a check for partner-lit tiles. Longer term the math starves it — the frontier grows one tile a week against a three-dig budget, so once the backlog is cleared the territory can't feed the ritual. Strong month-one, structurally thinning by month four.

> **N=2 judge (8/10):** Solo digs light tiles for both (the OR-gate-as-gift shape), the budget is joint with no per-person attribution, no history of wasted weeks exists anywhere in schema or UI, and silent per-week retirement makes suppression of painful eras unreadable — the relitigate-bad-weeks weapon is well defused. The only-rising completion number is safe. Residuals: the current week's visible remaining budget invites mid-week 'why haven't we dug' pressure (self-resolvable, since the asker can dig alone), and the use-or-lose cadence installs a weekly joint appointment that a jointly-busy week silently wastes — illegible, but a recurring small loss both can feel. 8.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** The unused memory_revisits table genuinely exists in the schema as claimed, week-scoped deterministic picks over plaintext-metadata tables are cheap, budget is a count query, and app_config covers tuning. Ding: 'mostly Rerun's engine re-aimed' — Rerun does not exist, so the selection engine and Keep/marks table must be built here, plus the map CustomPaint screen. ~1-1.5 weeks.

## Slow Light  —  C5 N9 F5 (Σ19)

*Some nights — rarely, unpredictably — Handoff's seal composer offers a second choice: seal it slow, and it surfaces in one of your partner's mornings on a date only the server knows; the repair of Drift's dead-pile flaw, and the one switching cost in the app that points FORWARD.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER (sender): opening the nightly seal composer occasionally reveals a 'let it drift' toggle — offer probability from app_config, roughly 1 night in 5 — so the composer itself becomes a slot the sender checks nightly. ACTION: seal content flagged slow; the server assigns release_on across 3-60 days (distribution in app_config) and shows the date to no one. VARIABLE REWARD (receiver): The Night is already the fixed morning appointment; most mornings carry last night's things or the Rerun fallback — some mornings open with a voice from three weeks ago, stamped only 'sealed weeks ago'. Fixed schedule, variable payout: the literal slot-machine core, laid onto the day's highest-intent open. VARIABLE REWARD (sender): the rare offer itself, plus a live Zeigarnik token — something of yours is in flight and you don't know which morning it lands. INVESTMENT: the in-flight stockpile — uninstalling doesn't just abandon the archive, it destroys letters that haven't arrived yet.

**Psychology.** Variable-ratio overlay on a fixed-interval appointment (Schull's machine-zone core: ritual play moments, unknowable payout timing); offer-scarcity FOMO on the sender side — the gacha-banner move; dual Zeigarnik open loops (sender: letter in flight; receiver: any morning might be a jackpot morning); loss aversion over undelivered mail, a genuinely new switching-cost class — the archive compounds backward, Slow Light compounds forward.

**Stolen from.** Gacha limited banners (Genshin, FGO): time-scarce offers are the industry's most reliable session-frequency lever, with revenue measurably concentrating inside banner windows. Slowly — the pen-pal app with distance-based delivery delay, 10M+ reported downloads — proved deliberately delayed delivery is experienced as added value, not friction. Natasha Schull's slot-machine research supplies the frame: compulsion lives in fixed ritual play against unknowable payout timing.

**N=2 adaptation.** This is the fix for the brief's own flagged failure — Drift's refill loop 'has no reward attached' and 'an empty pile is a dead feature': Slow Light makes the TICKET rare instead of relying on faith, so restocking is driven by offer-scarcity, not goodwill. Delay appreciates rather than cheapens content at N=2 — a three-week-old 'I miss you' proves the missing was durable. Release is gated to the receiver's on-device Handoff sunrise (profiles.timezone + ServerClock, the partner_sentence inputs), so nothing lands as a 3am push and delivery reveals nothing about the sender's clock.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** The offer-RNG doubles as an alibi — 'the app never offered me one' is unfalsifiable, so the absence of slow letters is illegible by construction, a Rule Zero positive. The real risk is stale context: a hot letter landing mid-fight, or after the relationship ends. The mitigation that keeps the compulsion: the sender can silently recall any in-flight item at any time with no trace on either side (Tucked's reclaim rule), and unpairing purges all in-flight items unopened; the 'sealed weeks ago' stamp stays vague so a landed letter cannot be forensically matched to a specific night.

**Build cost.** Small IF Handoff exists — it is a delta on Handoff exactly as Handoff is a delta on Rerun. One slow_seals table (couple_id, from_user, payload ref, sealed_night, release_on) where RLS shows the receiver the row only once release_on <= server-clock today (Sealed's row-existence arbitration — a patched client cannot peek) and a sender view that strips release_on; one toggle on the existing seal sheet; one WHERE clause in The Night's assembly query; app_config rows for offer probability and delay distribution. No pg_cron — release is evaluated at the morning fetch. No push, no native code.

**Composes with.** Extends Handoff (composer, The Night, sunrise computation) and borrows Sealed's server-arbitrated row visibility; the never-empty morning still falls back to Rerun/The Pull; a slow-sealed item that lands on an Almanac milestone day, or crosses with a partner's act in Crossings, is jackpot stacking for free.

> **Compulsion judge (5/10):** Both half-loops are real but low-volume: the sender's 1-in-5 composer offer makes the nightly seal a small slot machine, and the receiver's morning gains rare jackpot mornings — variable-ratio laid on a fixed appointment is the correct Schull core. But it sharpens Handoff's existing ritual rather than creating opens of its own, and in-flight volume (the actual payout rate) depends entirely on partners taking rare offers. An excellent 10% strengthening of someone else's loop.

> **N=2 judge (9/10):** Solo on both sides (sender seals alone, receiver's morning is a fixed solo appointment), timezone-native (release gated to the receiver's local sunrise), and the offer-RNG is a built-in alibi that makes the absence of slow letters unfalsifiable by construction — a rare mechanic where illegibility is the physics, not a policy. Silent recall plus unpair-purge handle the stale-context hazard. Docked one point because that hazard is real and the mitigation requires sender foresight: a hot letter landing mid-fight is a live grenade the design can only defuse if someone remembers to pull it back.

> **Feasibility judge (5/10):** Its own build note concedes the problem: 'Small IF Handoff exists' — and neither Handoff (seal composer, The Night morning surface) nor Rerun exists on this stack. The slow_seals table and release_on RLS are trivial, but the host surface must be built first, making this a multi-week dependency chain sold as a one-toggle delta. Weakest fit as specced.

## The Vintage  —  C3 N9 F7 (Σ19)

*On the couple's own anniversary each year, the year seals into an unrepeatable prestige artifact — a generative sigil struck from the year's aggregate metadata hash — and each sealed vintage permanently unlocks new mechanics across the app: New Game+ for a relationship.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: the anniversary — a real biological-grade date the couple already observes, not an app-invented cadence; the app builds quiet anticipation in the final month (the sigil is 'forming', shown only as an abstract shimmer, contents unknowable until struck). ACTION: none required — the vintage ALWAYS mints; presence in the year already did the work. Optionally, in the last week, each partner may privately seal one line into the vintage that the other cannot read until NEXT year's anniversary (a built-in Sealed-across-a-year, giving the following anniversary a guaranteed opening ritual). VARIABLE REWARD: the strike — the sigil's form is a hash-fingerprint of the year (which surfaces were touched, in what rhythm) and is genuinely unpredictable, plus the reveal of last year's sealed lines. INVESTMENT: the ladder — Vintage I, II, III accumulate as the app's only tenure display, and each unlocks permanent mechanical dowries: new Overlap window item types, new Tucked anchor locations, rare Pouch drop tiers, a new Homestead wing, next year's season theme palette (all delivered as app_config unlock tables — zero client update). Walking away from a three-vintage account means abandoning mechanics and artifacts that literally cannot exist on a fresh install for three more years.

**Psychology.** Loss aversion via unrepeatable time-gated exclusivity (the 2026 sigil can never be struck again — Duolingo's 'don't lose it' pull with nothing that can actually be lost through inactivity), escalating commitment/prestige laddering (MMO veteran rewards), and anticipation ritual (Spotify Wrapped's annual-mint spike: a once-a-year artifact reveal is one of consumer software's most reliable engagement events). The New Game+ unlocks convert tenure from sentiment into gameplay, which is the MMO school's core retention theorem.

**Stolen from.** FFXIV/WoW veteran & anniversary rewards (time-gated mounts/titles are among the most chased items in both games precisely because money cannot buy tenure). Spotify Wrapped (a yearly generative artifact from passive metadata, no user effort, producing a massive documented annual engagement spike and organic ritual). New Game+ (Stardew/roguelite meta-progression: permanent unlocks that make the NEXT cycle mechanically richer are the strongest known re-commitment device).

**N=2 adaptation.** The cadence is the couple's real anniversary (one additive column, couples.anniversary_on, set once during onboarding or from the earliest visits row) — the app borrows an existing sacred date instead of manufacturing one, so the appointment needs no enforcement. The sigil is struck from couple-pooled metadata only; both partners receive the identical artifact simultaneously, and the mutual year-sealed lines give each anniversary a symmetric, reciprocal opening moment where neither owes and both receive.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest risk: cross-year comparison as a trend meter — 'Vintage III is plainer than Vintage I; you're giving me less than you used to' (exactly the Hours refusal, on an annual clock). The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: richness-decoupling — the sigil's complexity derives from the hash's PATTERN, not from volume; any nonzero year strikes an equally ornate, differently-shaped sigil, so vintages differ like fingerprints, never like grades, and no mapping from activity level to visual weight exists to be read. The unrepeatability, the anticipation shimmer, and the reveal all survive; the meter is never born. Hard rules: no numeric year-stats on or near the sigil (no Wrapped-style '14,203 messages' — that IS the Hours number), and the mint is unconditional so a 'failed year' is not a representable state.

**Build cost.** Small — the smallest of the four. Migrations: couples.anniversary_on date (additive), vintages(couple_id, year_index, sigil_seed, struck_at), vintage_lines(couple_id, from_user, year_index, ciphertext, opens_year) with an RLS gate identical in shape to Sealed's (readable only when a vintages row for opens_year exists — server arbitrates by row existence, never content; E2EE-safe). app_config: unlock tables keyed by vintage count. Flutter: sigil generative painter (third consumer of the shared art budget), one anniversary screen, a small pre-anniversary Home shimmer. No push, no native code, no worker — the strike is client-side on first open past the date, idempotent.

**Composes with.** Extends Sealed across a year (the sealed lines are Sealed's RLS trick on an annual clock). Unlock dowries pay into Overlap (new window items — the brief's own noted thin-pool problem), Tucked (new anchors), The Pouch (rare drop tiers), Homestead (a new wing per vintage — visible age), and The Almanac (the year's volumes bind into the vintage as its spine). Timeline finally gets its replacement centerpiece for the condemned daysApartSinceLastVisit number: the vintage ladder.

> **Compulsion judge (3/10):** An annual mint with a one-month anticipation shimmer is a yearly engagement event, not a compulsion loop — Wrapped's December spike is the correct self-citation and also the ceiling. The New Game+ unlocks are real long-horizon retention infrastructure and the year-sealed lines are a lovely ritual, but nothing here produces an unprompted Tuesday open in March. Scores just above a mood ring because the final-month shimmer does condition some checking.

> **N=2 judge (9/10):** The mint is unconditional and requires no action from anyone — 'a failed year is not a representable state' is exactly the property this dimension demands, and richness-decoupling (pattern, not volume; no numeric stats ever) kills the cross-year trend meter before it's born. Annual cadence makes busy weeks irrelevant; timezones trivial. The one tooth: the optional year-sealed lines are symmetric only if both seal — an anniversary where one partner receives a line and the other sealed nothing is a small, quotable, once-a-year reciprocity gap the design doesn't address. That asymmetric reveal moment is the point off.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** Smallest schema footprint of its group: one additive column, two tables, Sealed-shape row-existence RLS, client-side idempotent strike, no cron or edge function. The sigil generative painter is the real cost, and the 'dowry' unlocks point mostly at unbuilt features (Overlap, Tucked, Homestead) — the core mint stands alone but loses much of its pitch without them. ~1 week.

## The Chain  —  C7 N3 F7 (Σ17)

*Sealed, serialized into a perpetual alternating correspondence: every reveal simultaneously plants the next locked link, so there is permanently one unopened thing of theirs on your phone and the only key is your own next blind deposit.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: a chain head sits on Home — a sealed shape with a shimmer, no age, no badge, no nag; you know something of theirs is inside and cannot preview it. ACTION: to open it you deposit your own link blind (photo, voice snip, or line — reusing the Sealed send mode), cost sunk before reward. VARIABLE REWARD: their link plays — a joke, a confession, a photo from Tuesday; unpredictable because they composed it not knowing what yours would be either. INVESTMENT: your deposit instantly becomes THEIR chain head, and the opened links append to a single scrollable chain object — an alternating correspondence artifact that grows for years, feeds Rerun, and whose length is endowment: walking away means orphaning the thing you built. The loop never resolves to 'done'; the chain is a permanently half-finished joint object.

**Psychology.** Zeigarnik effect (a permanently unfinished joint object), variable-ratio reward on each reveal, escalating commitment / sunk-cost via the growing artifact, and Cialdini reciprocity converted from guilt to self-interest by Sealed's blind-bid inversion — the pressure lands on the person who wants to OPEN, never on the person who hasn't replied.

**Stolen from.** Snapchat Streaks — the reciprocal alternating exchange is Snapchat's core dyadic loop; streaks drove daily snap volume and open frequency so reliably that Snap later SOLD streak restores ($0.99 Streak Restore), the strongest possible revealed-preference evidence of attachment to a reciprocal chain. Chain is a streak with the counter amputated and a curiosity payload (the sealed reveal) added in its place. Phones-by-the-pillow: a head that arrives overnight rides Handoff's The Night and is the first thing seen at wake, claiming the highest-intent minute of an LDR day.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest: a chain IS a turn-taking machine, and 'it's been your move for three days' is the obvious quote — this is the naked snap-back obligation the brief gutted, rebuilt. Weaponization: the faster partner uses chain state as evidence of neglect. The one load-bearing mitigation: the depositor's screen renders NO debt — after you deposit, your side shows only the finished links; no 'waiting for them', no pending state, no age anywhere. Only the person whose move it is ever sees a head, so the would-be creditor's UI contains nothing to quote while the debtor still feels the full pull of the sealed shape. Sealed's silent dissolve carries over: either partner can quietly end the chain; the links become a finished archive object and the other simply never sees a new head — indistinguishable from a slow week.

**Build cost.** Cheap if Sealed ships first, and buildable standalone otherwise. One table chain_links(id, couple_id, chain_id, seq, from_user, ciphertext, kind, opened_at) with the exact RLS shape the brief specced for sealed_halves: link N is SELECT-able by the receiver only when their own link N+1 row exists — Postgres arbitrates by row existence, never content, so it holds under E2EE and a patched client cannot peek. One Home head card, one chain-reader screen, reuse the chat/photo/voice composers via the Sealed send-mode plumbing. Roughly 2-3 days on top of Sealed; one migration.

**Composes with.** Sealed (same engine and RLS idiom — this is its 'go bigger'), Rerun (opened chains are premium candidate artifacts with built-in narrative), Handoff (a new head rides The Night), chat/photo/voice composers, closer_crypto for payload sealing.

> **Compulsion judge (7/10):** A permanently sealed thing from the one person you want, sitting on Home, unopenable without paying first — this is Snapchat's unopened-glyph itch (mobile's most compulsive icon) with a sunk-bid gate, and the loop is self-refueling since every open plants the next head. Craving moment: any idle glance at the phone while a head exists. Caps: compose friction on each deposit paces the loop to every day-or-two rather than multiple dailies, and a slow partner leaves you triggerless for stretches with nothing rendered to pull you back.

> **N=2 judge (3/10):** This is the snap-back obligation machine rebuilt, and the idea admits it: 'it's been your move for three days' is the quote. The UI shows the creditor nothing, but the structure hands them certainty anyway — they know a chain exists, they know a new head hasn't appeared, so the partner's absence is perfectly legible from memory with no receipt needed. Hard AND-gate: one partner's busy week stalls the entire loop while a sealed head sits on their Home as a designed daily guilt object ('the debtor still feels the full pull'). Silent dissolve is a good exit, but the core mechanic is a turn-taking debt engine whose stall state is an accusation waiting to be voiced. Structural evidence-of-neglect plus total busy-week death caps it at 3.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** The exists-subquery RLS (link N visible when your link N+1 row exists) matches the capsule refusal idiom and holds under E2EE. But the '2-3 days on top of Sealed' framing is optimistic: Sealed is unbuilt, so the send-mode plumbing and composer reuse must be built standalone — realistically ~a week. Mechanically clean otherwise: one table, one migration, existing composers.

## Aurora  —  C5 N5 F7 (Σ17)

*Server-scheduled sky weather: rare, unpredictable, minutes-long in-app events per couple — catchable alone, but igniting into a permanent joint artifact only when both were under the same sky.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: the standing possibility that the sky is on right now — no push by default, the check-in IS the habit (Animal Crossing meteor-shower behavior: players learn to look). A pg_cron worker pre-writes 3-6 events per week per couple at unpredictable times drawn from the couple's overlapping-wake window (computed from profiles.timezone), lasting 8-20 minutes each. ACTION: open the app while an event is live — Home is visibly transformed (a skin/light over home_screen.dart), and the photo/voice composer becomes 'leave something in this light'. VARIABLE REWARD, three-layered: (a) whether anything is live at all (variable-interval — most opens find ordinary sky, which is what makes checking compulsive); (b) event kind/skin varies (variable-ratio flavor); (c) the jackpot: next day-boundary, your catch either ignited — partner was under it too, and a joint 'we were both there' artifact appears, their capture beside yours — or nothing appears. The partner IS the slot machine. INVESTMENT: joint artifacts are stamped with the event and enter Rerun's candidate pool permanently, so every ignition makes future daily Reruns richer; the archive compounds with moments neither could have made alone.

**Psychology.** Compound schedule: variable-interval availability (the strongest known schedule for steady checking behavior) multiplied by a partner-contingent synchrony jackpot — the one variable reward the brief says is real at N=2 (the partner's timing) is made the top prize. Plus appointment scarcity: the event exists once, briefly, and cannot be caught up on.

**Stolen from.** BeReal — its entire engine is one unpredictable simultaneous window: 68% of users open within 3 minutes of the random daily notification and 55% are online simultaneously during it; that mechanic alone took it to 73.5M users at peak (Aug 2022). Secondary proof: MMO rare-spawn windows and Pokémon GO event days, which measurably spike DAU because the reward exists only at server-chosen times.

**N=2 adaptation.** BeReal's window is global and demands a capture (the brief rightly refused The Ping for exactly that). Aurora inverts both: events are per-couple, drawn from THEIR overlap window so synchrony is physically possible across timezones; capture is optional (being there is enough to ignite); and the crowd-jackpot is replaced by the partner-jackpot — at N=2 'were they under it too?' is the only unpredictable audience there is, and it is genuinely unpredictable because it depends on their mood, timing, and timezone. Both phones sleeping next to their owners means late-night events become the couple's private folklore ('the 1am aurora').

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest risk: a solo catcher KNOWS an event occurred and can quote it — 'there was an aurora last night and you weren't there' is a per-event guilt instrument. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: make non-ignition structurally ambiguous, three ways at once — (1) some events are server-flagged local-scope ('only over your city'), un-ignitable by design; (2) the second person gets a silent grace replay ('the sky is still glowing for you') until their next local day-boundary; (3) the ignition reveal is batched to the day-boundary, never live. A missing joint artifact is therefore indistinguishable between local-only / grace-pending / miss, no event history or schedule exists anywhere in the UI, and solo catches stay private by default — so the shared surface can only ever show presence, never absence. Expiry reads as weather because it IS weather.

**Build cost.** Medium. Server: one pg_cron job (pg_cron needs explicit install per project memory) + one small edge function generating a week of sky_events(couple_id, kind, starts_at, ends_at, seed, scope) ahead of time; sky_catches(event_id, user_id, payload_ciphertext, created_at) with the Sealed-idiom RLS gate — partner's catch row SELECT-able only when your own exists (exists-subquery, same trick the brief validated for Sealed). Tunables (events/week, duration, grace hours, scope mix) live in app_config, read at startup exactly like app_release in release_gate.dart — no client update ever needed to retune. Client: one Home overlay skin, composer reuse from features/photo, ServerClock window check, one joint-artifact card. Rollback: both tables additive; cron delete is the down migration.

**Composes with.** home_screen.dart (the sky lives there), features/photo composer (capture), ServerClock + profiles.timezone (window math partner_sentence.dart already does), RealtimeService Broadcast (optional live shimmer when both are in-app during an event), Rerun (joint artifacts feed its pool — this is Rerun's best supplier), and the disguise (a generic 'News' notification can never spoil what the sky was).

> **Compulsion judge (5/10):** The partner-synchrony jackpot is the best N=2 insight here, but the arithmetic kills the daily loop: 3-6 events/week at 8-20 minutes inside waking hours is roughly one live hour per week — a random open hits sky ~1% of the time, and there is no push by default. BeReal's 68%-within-3-minutes stat is evidence for the NOTIFICATION, which this design deliberately amputates; unprompted meteor-watching against odds this lean extinguishes in weeks for all but obsessive checkers. Beautiful folklore generator, starvation-schedule compulsion.

> **N=2 judge (5/10):** The top prize is hard AND-gated synchrony — the partner's presence IS the jackpot — which means during one partner's busy week the mechanic's whole point stalls, and the checking partner accumulates solo catches plus ambiguous non-ignitions that an anxious mind will read as absence regardless of the three-way ambiguity (local-scope, grace, batched reveal — genuinely clever, but ambiguity blunts proof, not feeling). The solo catcher still knows an event occurred, and 'there was an aurora and you weren't there' is a per-event guilt instrument the idea itself names. The solo layer (catch alone, skins) keeps it viable-ish and the shared surface only ever shows presence, which is why it's a 5 and not a 3 — but a mechanic that converts partner attention into the prize is structurally an attention-debt engine at N=2.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** All primitives exist: pg_cron, deployed edge functions to pattern-match, exists-subquery RLS, profiles even has wake_time/sleep_time for the overlap window, and app_config tuning fits the fleet constraint. Cost is honest medium: event generator, Home overlay skin, joint-artifact reveal, grace/scope logic — ~1-1.5 weeks, no forbidden elements.

## The Pouch  —  C4 N6 F7 (Σ17)

*A joint slot-machine economy: acts of affection sometimes drop coins into one shared pouch, and the pouch is the only way to unlock the cosmetic surfaces the app already ships — chat themes, camera filters, cover skins, game decks.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: sending anything at all — the act the couple already performs dozens of times a day. ACTION: the send. VARIABLE REWARD: a variable-ratio drop ('something fell out of that message' — a brief glint animation, no amount shown), rates and drop tables server-tuned via app_config; this is the literal Skinner schedule attached to an action with zero added cost, which is the purest nicotine in the entire design space. Amounts bank silently into the couple's single pouch in jittered daily batches. SPEND: coins unlock cosmetics for the COUPLE — a chat theme bought is instantly both partners' theme; ditto camera filter packs, disguise cover skins, truth/dare deck expansions, and Homestead furnishings — plus a slow luxury tier (commission a one-off generative artwork of a chosen memory). INVESTMENT: the wallet balance, the owned-cosmetics inventory, and the sunk identity of 'our theme, which we earned' — all of it worthless anywhere else.

**Psychology.** Variable-ratio reinforcement (the strongest schedule known) on the earn side + token economy + endowed ownership on the spend side. Critically it inverts Developing (the REFUSED idea): no progress bar, no withheld gift pointed at anyone, no completion target — just intermittent drops on behavior that was already happening, which is why it keeps the compulsion Developing had without its hostage structure.

**Stolen from.** Duolingo's gems economy (publicly credited, alongside streaks, in their S-1-era growth narrative for driving DAU habit; gems convert learning acts into a spendable balance with cosmetic/convenience sinks). MMO drop economies (WoW/Diablo: the drop-on-action glint is the most replicated compulsion loop in games). Fortnite proved cosmetics-only sinks sustain an economy indefinitely — no gameplay power needed, identity display is enough; at N=2 the 'display audience' is the one person whose opinion matters.

**N=2 adaptation.** One pouch, no trading, no gifting — at N=2 separate property is a coercion surface, so property is joint by design and 'gifting' collapses into joint unlocking. There is NO transaction ledger and NO earn history (a ledger is an activity log = absence made legible). The deeper N=2 move: self-knowledge obfuscation — because drops are probabilistic and amounts bank in jittered batches, a partner cannot compute even their OWN precise contribution, and subtraction ('balance minus mine equals your laziness') becomes arithmetic on two unknowns. Defeating differencing by blinding BOTH observers is the trick that makes a shared number safe at N=2.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest risks: 'earn tonight' (farming affection for coins) and 'you spent our coins without asking.' The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: abundance calibration + no ledger — prices sit low relative to ambient passive earn (tuned live via app_config) so scarcity never binds hard enough to make a demand worth voicing, and with no earn/spend history there is no evidence to prosecute either grievance with; the drop-glint thrill and the unlock ritual survive untouched. Hard rules on top: drops NEVER attach to Closer-module acts (echoing the Sealed whitelist — intimate content must never be coin-positive), and the two priciest sinks require both partners' keys to purchase (two-key spend as a commitment device, not a veto over pennies).

**Build cost.** Small-medium. Migrations: couple_wallet(couple_id, balance), couple_unlocks(couple_id, item_key, unlocked_at); drop logic in the same server-side triggers as Homestead bricks (one trigger family serves both — a patched client cannot mint). app_config: drop rates, prices, catalog. Flutter: glint animation on send paths, one pouch/shop sheet, and gating already-existing pickers — chat_theme_picker.dart, camera_filters.dart, truth_dare_deck.dart, disguise covers are ALL already built and become sinks with an unlock check each. No push, no native code.

**Composes with.** Turns four shipped-and-idle cosmetic systems (chat themes, camera filters, disguise cover skins, game decks) into the reward catalog for free. Funds Homestead furnishings (the two economies share one earn pipeline). Seasonal catalog rotation via app_config gives The Almanac's seasons a merchandising arm (this season's theme pack). Rerun's 'Keep' marks can earn a rare drop tier, paying the read path.

> **Compulsion judge (4/10):** Drop-glints on sends are the purest VR schedule here, but the design deliberately files down every tooth that makes token economies compulsive: no visible amounts, no balance legibility, jittered banking, and 'abundance calibration' explicitly tuned so scarcity never binds — a Skinner box engineered not to be felt is engineered not to compel. It drives sends (which happen in-app anyway), not opens, and the cosmetic sink catalog is finite. Correct safety trade, low compulsion residue.

> **N=2 judge (6/10):** A shared currency at N=2 is inherently a negotiation-and-blame surface, and two teeth survive the mitigations. First, spend attribution is certain by subtraction: an unlock is visible and joint, so if I didn't buy it, you did — 'you spent our coins without asking' has its evidence built into the feature (the unlock itself), no ledger required. Second, 'earn tonight' commodifies affection into farm work, and the defense — prices tuned low enough that demands aren't worth voicing — is a live-ops hope, not a structural guarantee; a config mistake re-arms it. Blinded contributions and no history are good work, and stakes are cosmetic-trivial, which is why this is 6 and not 3 — but it's the only idea whose safety depends on a tunable number staying tuned.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** Verified: all four claimed sinks already exist as code (chat_theme_picker.dart, camera_filters.dart, truth_dare_deck.dart, disguise covers), so the reward catalog is free. Wallet/unlocks tables and server-side drop triggers are simple; glint animation, shop sheet, unlock-gating each picker, and the two-key spend ceremony add breadth. ~1 week; docked from 8 for depending on Homestead's trigger family framing and app_config creation.

## Fireflies  —  C4 N6 F7 (Σ17)

*Variable-ratio catchables that exist ONLY while the couple is live together — during a call, watch-together, or breath session a firefly drifts across both screens in sync, either partner's tap catches it for both, and species collect into a shared jar that is a portrait of how they spend time together.*

**Loop.** Trigger: being in any joint live session (which was already happening for its own sake) plus the learned knowledge that a spawn could come at any minute. Action: peripheral vigilance and a single tap — near-zero cost, and the interruption itself is a shared moment ('there's one!' mid-call, both screens celebrating in sync via the session's Broadcast channel). Variable reward: spawn timing (deterministic from hash(session_id + minute_window) so both phones render it identically with no server round-trip — but unpredictable to the humans), species (keyed to session kind: call-species, watch-species, breath-species — each activity grows its own line in the jar), and rarity (pure randomness at a constant per-minute rate). Investment: the jar fills against silhouetted species sets; each catch is stamped with its session and leaves the session-end row the brief endorsed emitting — so the catch is simultaneously a collectible AND an archive artifact ('caught during the call where we...') that feeds Rerun and The Mint later. The teeth: a slot-machine undertone under every call — 'five more minutes, one might come' — converting the dyad's scarcest resource, simultaneity, into collectible capital.

**Psychology.** Variable-ratio reinforcement layered ON TOP of intrinsically motivated behavior — the strongest compound in the reinforcement literature, because the reward accompanies rather than replaces the thing you already wanted to do — plus collection completion (species sets), plus documented session-extension via random-spawn anticipation.

**Stolen from.** Pokémon GO (the definitive proof that variable spawns layered on an intrinsically valuable activity extend real-world behavior — walking sessions measurably lengthened, time-boxed Community Day spawns moved millions of people's schedules; unverified precise figures, but the mechanism is the best-documented in the genre), Animal Crossing's time-and-season-gated bugs/fish (players demonstrably schedule real hours around rare spawns — which is exactly the lever we deliberately REMOVE, see coercion).

**N=2 adaptation.** The spawn machine is structurally incapable of firing on an absent partner: spawns exist only when both are present, so nothing is ever missed BECAUSE someone was away — there was no firefly, and no record disagrees (misses leave zero trace, no missed-spawn counter exists). Either partner's tap catches for both with no per-person catch count — being the one who caught it is a micro-gift, not a stat. Species are keyed to the couple's own activity mix, so the completed jar is a portrait of the relationship's texture (a couple who only calls has a different jar than a couple who watches). At exactly two users, this is the honest answer to 'no crowd = no variable reward': the crowd is replaced by time-together itself as the slot machine's coin.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest: session-extension is the point, and it cuts both ways — 'stay on, a rare one might come' aimed at a partner who needs to sleep, or clock-gated species incentivizing demanded 4am calls (the Animal Crossing lever, which is why it's banned here). The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: NO species is gated on wall-clock hour, and spawn rate is constant per-minute — linear, never superlinear — with rarity from randomness rather than duration, so a 40-minute call and a 3-hour call have the same expected rarity per minute and marathon-grinding is never dominant. The 'five more minutes' pull survives (more minutes = more rolls) while the 'wake up at 4am' and 'we must do marathon calls' levers never exist.

**Build cost.** Small-medium; the cost is breadth (Tucked-shaped). An overlay widget injected into three surfaces — call screen, watch (reels co-watch), breath — listening on the session's existing Broadcast channel; spawn computed on-device from the shared session seed, catch = one broadcast message + insert catches(couple_id, species_id, session_kind, session_ref, caught_at) with couple-scoped RLS. Sprites in-APK; spawn tables, per-minute rate, and species manifest in app_config (tunable + extensible with no APK). Jar/collection screen. No edge functions, no push, no scheduler.

**Composes with.** call, watch/reels, breath, and Overlap's Window if built (the fourth spawn surface); emits the session-end rows the brief's 'Hours' verdict explicitly endorsed (memory, not metric); catches feed Rerun's candidate pool and mint as cards in The Mint; caught fireflies can be released into Understory's garden as ambient light — the four ideas form one economy: Fireflies mints live time, The Mint mints the past, the Atlas maps it, Understory is where it all visibly lives.

> **Compulsion judge (4/10):** Judged on producing opens, it structurally can't: spawns exist only inside joint live sessions, so the mechanic never fires on a closed app — it garnishes and extends sessions that already happened for their own sake. The 'five more minutes, one might come' pull is real session-extension compulsion, and catch-for-both is charming, but there is no trigger toward the app, no loss (misses untraceable by design), and the collection fills only as fast as the couple already calls. A multiplier with no base to multiply on quiet days.

> **N=2 judge (6/10):** The safety engineering is near-perfect: spawns are structurally incapable of firing on an absent partner, misses leave zero trace, no per-person catch counts, no clock-gated species, linear per-minute rates — nothing here can be quoted, counted, or blamed. But it fails the other half of the dimension outright: it is the only idea with no solo path at all — the entire mechanic exists only during joint live sessions, so one partner's busy week doesn't degrade it, it switches it off completely. It's dormant-not-dying (nothing decays, no residue), which saves it from the bottom, and the silhouette sets exert a mild demand-shaped pull toward specific joint activities ('we need breath-species'). Excellent safety, structurally AND-gated viability: 6.

> **Feasibility judge (7/10):** No server scheduler at all: spawns derive from the shared session seed (watch_sessions/call session ids exist), catches are one broadcast message + one couple-RLS insert, and RealtimeService.broadcast is shipped. Cost is breadth — overlay integration into three live surfaces (call, watch, breath) each with its own screen state, plus sprite assets. ~1 week; solidly feasible, not a days job.

## The Almanac  —  C? N? F? (Σ0)

*Server-scheduled seasons (8-12 weeks, authored as app_config/table rows — no client update needed) each open a small shelf of themed slots the couple fills with artifacts old and new; at season's end whatever exists is minted into a permanent, immutable illustrated volume on the couple's shelf.*

**Loop.** TRIGGER: appointment scarcity — the season is live NOW and mints on a fixed date; plus the open-loop itch of unfilled slots (Zeigarnik). ACTION: either partner fills any slot alone — 'a photo of a sky', 'a voice note of laughter', 'an answer to: what did I not thank you for', 'something from before this year' (that last slot type is fillable ONLY from the archive, making an archive dive compulsory — Rerun extended from a passive card into an active hunt). Season opens with 1-2 slots ALREADY filled, auto-detected from plaintext metadata (e.g. 'a photo taken on a Sunday' matched client-side from created_at) — endowed progress. VARIABLE REWARD: two-armed — (a) the partner's fills appear unannounced, so opening the season screen is a slot-machine whose reels are the partner (the brief's scarce-unpredictable-resource, spent correctly); (b) the mint itself: the volume's generative layout is unpredictable until it renders. INVESTMENT: the shelf. Volume 1 is nice; a shelf of six volumes spanning two years is a physical-feeling library of the relationship that cannot be exported, re-earned, or replaced — each was mintable only in its season.

**Psychology.** Appointment mechanics + set-completion compulsion + endowed progress effect (Nunes & Drèze's classic result: pre-stamped loyalty cards nearly doubled completion, 34% vs 19%) + time-gated exclusivity on the mint. The season deadline manufactures urgency at N=2 without needing a crowd, and the partner-fills-as-reels converts partner unpredictability into the reward schedule.

**Stolen from.** Battle-pass seasons (Fortnite/Diablo/CoD: season boundaries produce documented return spikes and the season-exclusive cosmetic — 'never earnable again' — is the industry's most reliable re-engagement lever). Animal Crossing's museum and Stardew's community-center bundles prove slot-filling collection against a themed board is compulsive solo/duo, no leaderboard required. Pokémon Go Special Research proves timed themed task-sets drive lapsed-user return.

**N=2 adaptation.** Slot boards are small (5-8) because two people fill them. Any slot is solo-fillable — no slot ever requires both partners, so no slot can become a demand on a specific person. Fills are not attributed on the board (no avatar chips); at N=2 you often know anyway, but the SURFACE never testifies. Season close uses the later partner's local midnight (the RoutineRepository.today() two-timezone pattern) so no one loses a mint to timezone arithmetic. Seasons are authored server-side, so the sideload fleet gets fresh content forever — this is the feature that most exploits the new app_config capability.

**Coercion risk + mitigation.** Honest risk: the deadline weaponized — 'fill the shelf tonight, the season ends Sunday,' i.e. Developing's hostage shape sneaking back in via calendar. The one mitigation that keeps the compulsion: the mint is unconditional and gap-free — the volume renders beautifully at ANY fill level (a 2-item season makes a sparse, lovely page, not a failure), no completion percentage is ever shown during or after, skipped seasons leave NO gap because volumes are titled by theme, never numbered ('The Rain Volume', not 'Vol. 7'), so absence produces no artifact of absence. The deadline keeps its pull (the exclusive mint still exists only in-season) but there is nothing to fail, hence nothing to prosecute.

**Build cost.** Medium. Migrations: seasons(id, opens_at, closes_at, theme, slots jsonb) — server-authored rows, RLS-readable by all; season_fills(couple_id, season_id, slot_key, target_kind, target_id, ciphertext note) — E2EE-safe: fills reference encrypted artifacts, slot matching happens client-side, server sees only which slot_keys are filled. Volumes render client-side into volumes(couple_id, season_id, seed, minted_at) + a generative layout painter (shares art budget with Homestead). Flutter: season screen, shelf screen, a 'send to season' affordance on gallery/chat media/prompt surfaces. No push required (a quiet Home chip in the final week).

**Composes with.** Rerun (archive-only slots make revisiting compulsory; Rerun's daily card can flag 'this fits the open season'); gallery, chat media, daily_prompt (Again answers are prime fills), memory_threads, capsule; The Pouch (season-themed cosmetic catalog rotation); The Vintage (year's volumes bind into the vintage — see idea 4); Handoff (a sealed night-thing can be dropped directly into a slot).
